David Goldberg (Berlin/Munich) explores how contemporary systems shape perception and collective imagination. In his video works, installations, and performances, he examines the tension between technological progress and ethical responsibility, between economic growth and ecological necessity. His practice investigates how acceleration and efficiency, once promises of advancement, have turned into forces of disconnection. Drawing on traces of past ideologies and aesthetic residues, Goldberg reveals how patterns of control and belief persist within today’s visual and digital cultures. A central concern of his work is the use of narrative structures instead of statistical forms of communication—employing story and embodiment to translate abstract systems into perceptible experience. By doing so, he exposes their constructed nature and invites contemplation on how perception is guided, shaped, and reinforced by the systems that produce it.
